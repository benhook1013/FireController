# Unfinished review obligations during adoption

## Implementation status

The service and bound CLI implement source-bound finding, route and unsettled-result holds, revision-guarded resolution, policy linkage and minimum-only round adoption. Focused runtime and canonical backup checks cover these boundaries. The offline control preparer accepts a strictly bound carried frame and native unchanged-preview receipts; the live acquisition command still requires an obligation-clear frame. A selected complete source-owner carried frame has been composed from conserved canonical collected audits and applied to a hosted installation: twenty-three obligations, seventy-six adopted channels and two explicitly deferred channels. Installed readback and original-key recovery pass; final full-data recovery and worker resume remain separate. Native hold support alone does not establish source completeness or settle an unfinalized source result.

## Preserve the working rules

A project can move to this service while useful review work remains unfinished. It must not finish reviews in the predecessor merely to satisfy the importer, lose an outstanding correction or route, or receive fabricated native review credit. Keep the predecessor fenced and its exact retained records available privately.

Optional source tooling exports the selected effective policy and its current unresolved obligations from one sealed source. The export distinguishes current obligations from incomplete historical attempts and unselected history. It preserves source identities, original attribution, current disposition and the precise selected PRs affected by each obligation. Unknown scope or source audit failure remains an explicit refusal. Paused workers, a completed provider attempt, exhausted rounds and channel closure never establish clearance.

## Carried obligations

The native service accepts generic source-bound obligations through an administrator preview/apply facility, independently of display history and captured native evidence. A carried obligation identifies its source namespace and immutable key, repository and snapshot digest, kind (finding, route or result), retained summary and evidence references, and explicitly affected PRs. The service retains private original provenance. It does not interpret imported text as instructions, merge authority or proof of current-code coverage.

Open obligations hold new review admission and queue progression for their affected PRs in both channels. The guard applies before provider preparation and again at transactional operation admission and final dispatch. A finished grant, existing capture, channel closure, head change, reordered queue or another channel cannot bypass it. Unrelated PRs and projects retain their existing behavior. Local assessment and browser views explain the hold with bounded counts and a deliberate detail read; ordinary calls do not print the imported ledger.

Preview is read-only and binds the complete selected batch to the destination instance, project, administrator, worker and expected revision. Apply rechecks scope and current authority and atomically records obligations, provenance, receipt and compact events. An original-key replay returns the original result. Reimport cannot reopen a resolved obligation, erase a native action or silently replace changed source facts. Omission is additive, never deletion or resolution. Admission refuses conflicting provenance. A protective hold may arrive during queued or prepared work; final dispatch guards must observe it. It does not cancel or relabel an existing intent, or prevent consuming and finalizing a result that was already posted.

Resolution is an explicit revision-guarded action retaining actor, reason and evidence pointers. The worker must establish that the carried obligation has been addressed; importing it or merging its original PR is insufficient. Preserve original source state and later native resolution as distinct facts. Resolution neither changes original finalized review counts nor creates a captured review, comparison coverage, CI observation or merge permission.

A positively identified terminal source result whose eventual adjudication can still change remaining allowance or dry streak has an unsettled policy boundary as well as unfinished findings. Retain a separate `result` obligation alongside its finding obligations, and defer numeric control preparation for that unsettled channel. Finding resolution cannot remove the result hold. Before explicitly resolving the result obligation, the resolving worker must verify evidence of its final counting effect and preserve the correct remaining policy at the destination. Resolution records that proof; it does not apply policy or count a completion automatically. Do not count preliminary zero accepted findings as a finalized dry round, or import today's allowance and later release the hold while ignoring the pending completion. An active, ambiguous or unidentified external request is not a terminal result and cannot be made quiescent by importing a hold.

Admission validates each selected obligation, target list and resolution in bounded pages. Within one facts read, at most sixty-four successfully validated source namespaces are reused; additional namespaces are validated normally, and no cache survives the read. A proven change between the project repository and retained source repository produces a protective `repository_changed` hold. Missing or malformed retained authority remains unavailable and never supplies dispatch permission or completion credit.

## Bound commands and authority

Import preview/apply use the private administrator profile. Listing, reading, retargeting and resolving use the selected project profile; retargeting and resolution require ordinary project write permission and a valid project worker, without an administrator or Overseer handoff. Ordinary commands remain compact and provider-free:

```sh
fc reviews obligations adopt preview --file /private/obligations.json
fc reviews obligations adopt apply --file /private/obligations.json --preview SHA256 --key import-obligations
fc reviews obligations list --pr 42 --status open
fc reviews obligations read OBLIGATION_ID
fc reviews obligations retarget OBLIGATION_ID --expected-revision 1 --pr 43 --reason "Move receiving work" --key retarget-obligation
fc reviews obligations resolve OBLIGATION_ID --expected-revision 1 --reason "Correction verified" --evidence COMMIT_OR_PROOF --key resolve-obligation
```

The version-one batch contains `source: {namespace, repository_id, snapshot_sha256}`, `expected_revision` and one to one hundred `obligations`. Each obligation contains `source_key`, `kind` (`finding`, `route` or `result`), `affected_pull_numbers`, `summary`, `evidence` and `original_status` (`pending` or `open`). Original evidence may be an explicit empty array when unavailable; a native resolution requires at least one evidence pointer. Each normalized original JSON record is bounded to 32 KiB, each combined normalized resolution or retarget request to 16 KiB, and the complete normalized apply wrapper to 256 KiB. These encoded bounds include escaping. Full-detail reads have a 96 KiB HTTP response budget in the shared CLI/MCP client, preserving previously accepted originals, resolutions and retargets without tightening their limits. The three retained content blocks contribute at most 64 KiB; replacing request revisions with retained revisions and adding attribution, source identity and current scope adds less than 16 KiB. A conservative overhead bound allows eight 128-byte IDs, a 200-byte namespace, the 64-byte digest and three 64-byte timestamps at up to six encoded bytes per input byte, 101 ten-digit affected PRs plus separators, ten 20-byte numeric values, and 2 KiB for field names and JSON punctuation. The resulting bound is below 80 KiB, leaving headroom within the finite 96 KiB transport cap. Detail contains only the latest retarget, not its unbounded history. Backup validation retains the original admission limits. The CLI validates the owner-private regular input before reading credentials or making a request. Preview changes no state; apply binds its exact normalized content and preserves original-key recovery after lost output or later resolution.

`list` returns identity, status, revision and affected PRs, without expanding summaries or evidence. Its 512 KiB response budget accommodates one hundred items with maximally escaped 200-byte namespace and source keys and up to 101 affected PRs per route. `read` deliberately returns retained originals, current scope, the latest retarget if present and any native resolution. The HTTP owner is `/api/v1/projects/{project_id}/reviews/adoption-obligations`, with `GET`, `GET /{id}`, `POST /preview`, `POST /apply`, `POST /{id}/retarget` and `POST /{id}/resolve`; import preview/apply require administrator authority and a valid project worker. Resolution requires project write permission and a valid project worker, rechecked before the first mutation and original-key replay; read-only access cannot resolve obligations. Reads and mutations retain the ordinary short command budget and never call a review provider.

### Refusals and recovery

The native HTTP contract uses the following codes for guarded changes and source-policy linkage. A matching preview hash is not a reservation: apply rechecks current admission guards. Recover lost output with the original key and unchanged request; a retained receipt does not bypass current authentication or permission checks.

| Code | Where it applies | Caller action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `adoption_obligations_conflict` | `POST /{id}/retarget` or `POST /{id}/resolve`: stale `expected_revision` or an obligation that is no longer open. | Re-read the obligation and its current revision/status. Reassess whether work remains before submitting a new guarded action; do not resolve or retarget an already resolved obligation. |
| `adoption_obligations_conflict` | Import preview/apply: source revision, repository, snapshot or retained original facts disagree. Linked round adoption: retained source snapshot/revision mismatch, including a source revision advancing after preview. | Inspect the retained source and prepared document. Correct a stale revision only after checking the current facts, then preview again. Stop if immutable provenance disagrees; do not rewrite source facts to force admission. |
| `import_preview_mismatch` | Obligation or round-adoption apply: the exact normalized document or bound destination/actor/worker differs from its preview. | Preview the intended document with the intended binding again, then apply that unchanged document and returned hash. |
| `review_round_revision_conflict` | Round-adoption preview/apply: the PR/channel is already configured, including a control created after preview. | Read the existing control and stop this adoption rather than overwrite it; see [round adoption](review-round-controls.md#prepared-operational-adoption). |
| `review_round_adoption_conflict` | Round-adoption preview/apply: the prepared repository differs from the destination or the source rule was already adopted. | Verify source/destination identity and existing adoption. Stop rather than reuse adopted provenance or force a repository match. |
| `validation_failed` | Invalid native input, including a version-two policy link whose `namespace` differs from the prepared control's `source`. | Correct the document against the native schema and verified provenance; preview changed import/adoption input again. |
| `not_found` | The selected project, worker, obligation or linked retained source is absent in the bound scope. | Verify the binding and identifier. A policy link requires its matching obligation source to be retained in that project first; do not treat absence as clearance. |
| `repository_changed` | A retained obligation source belongs to a different repository from the project's current binding. | Stop and have the repository/source binding verified. Retrying with a newer revision does not repair provenance. |
| `adoption_obligation_not_route` | Retargeting an open `finding` or `result` obligation after its revision/status guard passes. | Stop retargeting it. Only `route` obligations can change receiver; resolution still requires actual completion evidence. |
| `forbidden` | Required administrator/project permission or worker attribution is missing, including on exact-key replay. | Stop until the required authority and valid project-worker binding are available. A previous receipt does not authorize its retrieval after permission loss. |
| `unauthorized` | Authentication is missing, invalid, expired or revoked, including on exact-key replay. | Restore valid authentication before retrying the unchanged original request/key; do not replace the key to bypass revocation. |

## Policy and obligation linkage

Operational policy adoption can proceed when the source audit establishes clearance, or when its complete selected unresolved set has been retained as matching native holds. The latter is transfer, not clearance. Bind policy and obligation provenance to the same repository, source namespace and sealed snapshot, and verify the matching retained obligation revision during policy admission. A helper must not change `obligations_clear` to true or drop source obligations to obtain a policy payload. Display-only imports and an arbitrary note are not substitutes for enforced holds.

Version-two prepared controls can include `obligations: {namespace, snapshot_sha256, revision}`. The namespace must equal the prepared control's `source`; the repository and exact current retained source revision must match at preview and apply. This snapshot digest is separate from `source_sha256`, which can bind combined source, helper and frame provenance. Later additive imports preserve the older immutable policy reference, while a stale preview cannot adopt against a newer source revision. Version-one payloads retain their existing shape and meaning.

The source-owner carried-frame extension retains `projection_complete: true`, `quiescent: true` and the exact sealed repository/snapshot binding while stating `obligations_clear: false`. Its complete selected obligation ledger must agree with the canonical source audit; only positively identified findings, routes and terminal unsettled results can be transferred. Unknown request identity, mutable external work or missing scope proof remain refusals. A channel whose source counting effect is unsettled is explicitly deferred and produces no numeric prepared control. The matching result obligation keeps that PR held while other proved channels and coordination data can transfer. Numerical allowance never authorizes a review or establishes current-code coverage. Preserve the source's selected parent graph and qualify the destination's fresh comparison guards before enabling execution; a known branch-preparation condition must not disappear merely because its counts can transfer. Unknown identity or active external work remains a refusal. The offline preparer implements this carried-frame admission. The live acquisition command retains its clearance-only format; complete carried collection remains a separate source-owner responsibility.

Preserve effective remaining minimum, dry streak, reached taper and explicit closure. A source policy with no hard maximum retains an explicitly absent maximum; zero remains exhausted and must not mean unlimited. Ordinary project-write grants continue to require an explicit finite maximum. Unbounded adoption does not permit concurrent dispatch past the existing channel reservation, change taper, waive minimums, or overflow completion accounting. Reads and the browser distinguish an absent ceiling from a finite remaining count.

The explicit `maximum_unbounded` flag represents that absent ceiling. It is omitted when false, preserving ordinary finite records. When true, numeric `maximum` (or prepared `maximum_remaining`) is zero only as a storage placeholder; the flag supplies the unbounded meaning. A false or absent flag with zero retains its existing exhausted meaning. A new finite grant clears the flag. Completion and dry counters remain exact within the shared JavaScript-safe integer bound and refuse further advancement before overflow; this implementation bound is not a substituted review allowance.

## Current receivers of carried routes

An open carried `route` obligation can change its receiving PR without resolving the work. Use `reviews obligations retarget ID --expected-revision N --pr NUMBER --reason TEXT --key KEY`, or `--unassigned` instead of `--pr` to keep it visible in the unassigned attention backlog. MCP `review_obligation_retarget` takes `obligation_id`, `expected_revision`, explicit `target_pull_number` (zero means unassigned), `reason` and `idempotency_key`. The native HTTP operation is `POST /api/v1/projects/{project_id}/reviews/adoption-obligations/{id}/retarget` with `{expected_revision,target_pull_number,reason}` and the ordinary explicit key header. It requires project write access and an bound project worker; no provider work or review request occurs.

The service derives the current affected PRs, preserving original source PR scope and replacing only the receiving target. Detail reads return top-level `affected_pull_numbers` for current scope and optional `retarget` attribution: revision, receiver, reason, acting principal/worker and change time. `original` remains immutable imported provenance. List filters, review holds, routed-work attention and the detail view use that same current scope. Importer `actor_id` and `worker_id` are historical attribution, not a separately assigned worker.

Retargeting keeps the obligation open, advances its guarded revision and records the change. Selecting the same current receiver is an unchanged no-op; it does not rewrite the reason or history. Resolution uses the current obligation revision and still requires real evidence. A merged or closed PR, a new receiver, or an empty backlog receiver is never proof of completion. `finding` and `result` obligations cannot be retargeted through this operation. No duplicate native route or job ledger is created.

The original receiver is identified only by exactly one canonical `target-pr:N` entry in the original evidence, where `N` is a positive PR number, or `target-pr:unassigned`. Missing, malformed or multiple receiver markers leave the original receiver unknown; array position does not identify it. Retargeting always starts from the immutable original `affected_pull_numbers`: remove the explicitly identified positive original receiver, preserve every other original PR, then add the new positive receiver once and sort. Thus `{2,4}` with `target-pr:4` becomes `{2,6}` when retargeted to 6, while `{3}` without a marker becomes `{3,4}` when retargeted to 4. A target of `0` adds no receiver and removes only a known positive original receiver; unknown original scope is preserved.
