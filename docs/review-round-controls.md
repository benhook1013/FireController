# Per-PR review rounds

## Implementation status

The native service, bound CLI and MCP implement explicit per-PR/channel additional-round grants, minimum and maximum requirements, taper, closure/reopening and persistent native completion accounting. Source preparation and final admission recheck the grant independently of captured coverage; policy/readiness and browser assessment reads show outstanding round requirements. Backup validation preserves the control history and native completion links. Focused checks are recorded below. The service owns channel-front selection for one explicit start command. Prepared operational adoption and the offline source-helper adapter implement the selected carry boundary; acquiring a complete canonical source frame and real-project adoption remain separate steps. A grant alone performs no provider action and does not weaken the separate [review policy](reviews.md#project-policy).

## Operational controls

Hosted and CLI have independent per-PR scheduling state and independently advance through eligible work in queue priority order. A cooldown or active review in one channel does not hold the other channel. Minimum and maximum count additional completed rounds. Equal values express an exact count; the CLI's `--exact` is only shorthand for those equal bounds. A maximum permits early taper once the minimum is satisfied.

New execution enrollment atomically initializes absent PR/channel rules: Hosted minimum two, maximum three and taper one; CLI minimum two, maximum six and taper three. Reordering, pausing or changing capability leaves existing enrolled pairs untouched, including older unconfigured allocations. Explicit or adopted controls installed before enrollment are preserved. Import verified source-owned controls before enrolling their PR/channel; no temporary default needs replacement.

Default early taper is one eligible Hosted round with zero accepted findings, or three consecutive eligible CLI rounds with zero accepted findings. A selected minimum still applies. A useful accepted correction resets the applicable dry streak; failed, incomplete, ambiguous and rate-limited attempts cannot supply successful dry rounds. Preserve the distinction between exhausted allowance, taper completion and explicit closure.

Closing a channel affects only that PR/channel. Every explicit additional-round grant reopens that channel, including after taper or explicit closure, while preserving its earned streak and completed history. A maximum-only grant permits another eligible result before taper is evaluated again; it does not silently increase the requested minimum. An explicit minimum still requires that many additional completed rounds. No implicit project-wide closure or reopening follows from a local change.

Candidate, base, effective-parent or history movement after start preserves earned PR/channel completion, completed rounds, allowance history, taper, limits and closure. It never automatically reopens the channel or requires more rounds. Original captures still identify only the code actually reviewed. Extra review is deliberately requested through the existing controls. Closing, tapering or exhausting a channel does not erase unfinalized evidence, accepted/routed findings or imported obligations across any head, establish current CI or authorize a merge. Required channels and explicit round requirements remain enforced; current source readiness is a separate assessment.

## Native control semantics

A per-PR/channel grant is an explicit project write operation, separate from administrator-owned project-wide required channels. Requested additional maximum is between one and 1,000; additional minimum is between zero and that maximum. The service stores checked cumulative stopping thresholds while preserving consumed counts, the accounting epoch, imported provenance and original operation attribution. Cumulative counters stay within 9,007,199,254,740,991. Historical controls retain their original representation and proof; an explicit edit moves the current rule to cumulative semantics. Existing unconfigured allocations retain their stored control state; newly enrolled pairs receive the product plan above. Earned completion follows the [PR/channel rule](reviews.md#pr-and-channel-completion) in either case.

One eligible completion has a completed native operation/attempt, validated attributable complete capture and finalized source counts. An operation counts once, independently of whether its accepted findings have been fixed. Original finalized accepted counts determine its historical streak contribution; later decision/fix corrections remain separate obligations and do not rewrite consumed rounds. Failed, rate-limited and incomplete attempts consume no completed round. Queued, preparing, posting, active and uncertain work occupies capacity. An attributable complete terminal result awaiting finalization also occupies its original capacity, without receiving completion credit early.

Discovery completes when the maximum is consumed, or an eligible completion since the latest additional-round grant reaches the minimum and the retained clean streak meets taper. The immutable grant's completed count marks that boundary; no new accounting epoch or reset of earned streak is needed. A maximum-only grant of two on a tapered channel therefore permits up to two more rounds: one clean completion may taper again using the preserved streak, while a completion with accepted findings resets the streak normally. A minimum of two additional rounds prevents stopping until those two complete even if the streak already meets its target. An accepted completion resets the cumulative rule's streak, including a result already posted before closure or taper. Previously retained grants and exact-key receipts keep their original meaning; deployment alone never reopens a channel.

Close stops new dispatch, including queued/prepared actions at their final guard, and does not cancel a possibly posted action. A posted result can still be captured, adjudicated and counted once with its original operation binding. Changing limits includes underway and complete-unfinalized work: “one more” includes that round. To require a round after it, wait for its finalization before granting the additional requirement. The service refuses a new maximum smaller than unavoidable occupied work. Exact-key replay never purchases another round or changes the retained request.

The first explicit conversion of a legacy rule reconstructs only its clean streak from immutable, already-counted current-epoch completions. This handles older rules that froze the displayed streak after taper: all-clean results preserve earned progress, and a recorded accepted result resets it. It adds no completion credit, changes no original capture/counts and leaves the previous history bytes intact. Backup replay validates the same evidenced transition. Older past epochs and imported history remain historical evidence, not new native completions.

Per-PR changes use their own revision and actor attribution rather than changing the project policy revision or holding unrelated PRs. Requests retain their original grant binding and recheck the current same-epoch limits, closure and obligations before preparation and provider dispatch. Both source preparation and final transactional admission allow another round after earned PR/channel completion only while the bound grant still requires discovery. They preserve pending-capture/finding obligations, source identity, current authority, provider limits and reservations. For ordinary stacked progression, an explicitly finished grant replaces the predecessor single-capture requirement; outstanding evidence/fix obligations still hold dependent work. An [explicit out-of-order request](reviews.md#explicit-requests-outside-queue-order) skips this scheduling requirement while preserving the target's own obligations and real effective-parent comparison. Closure or exhausted allowance without validated native completion remains a separate readiness limitation and never manufactures earned credit; later Git movement alone does not create that limitation.

Control reads are local, compact and provider-free. Project write mutations use the ordinary authorization, expected revision, idempotency and atomic event/receipt rules. Their compact change events identify the affected PR/channel resource and its revision; the durable event cursor remains the delivery deduplication identity. The explicit run selection below retains caller authority, rechecks current authority and provider hold, and selects each channel independently. Storing a grant does not authorize provider calls. See [prepared operational adoption](#prepared-operational-adoption) for the implemented conversion boundary; acquiring a canonical source frame and qualifying a real-project adoption remain separate steps.

## Bound commands and API

`fc reviews help` lists local round-control discovery. `fc reviews rounds --help` identifies the existing settings read, and `fc reviews rounds read --help` explains its selectors and counters without loading profiles or contacting the service. The read selects one explicit PR and Hosted or CLI channel; inspecting both channels uses two reads.

Commands use the selected project profile with ordinary project write permission. Workers can grant, close and reopen a PR/channel directly; no Overseer identity or administrator handoff is required. Read-only credentials and read-only browser sessions cannot change controls. Reads retain the ordinary five-second budget and perform no provider work. `--min` defaults to zero; `--exact` is exclusive with `--min` and `--max`. A close requires the current revision and omits allocation fields.

```sh
fc reviews rounds read --pr 42 --channel cli
fc reviews rounds grant --pr 42 --channel cli --expected-revision 1 --max 8 --key cli-42-grant
fc reviews rounds grant --pr 42 --channel hosted --expected-revision 1 --min 2 --max 2 --key hosted-42-grant
fc reviews rounds close --pr 42 --channel cli --expected-revision 2 --key cli-42-close
```

`GET /api/v1/projects/{project_id}/reviews/rounds/{pull_number}/{channel}` returns the compact configured state, revision/epoch, bounds, consumed/dry counts, taper and closure. An unconfigured read explicitly returns `configured:false` with revision zero. Project-authorized write `POST` at the same route accepts `expected_revision`, `action` (`grant` or `close`), and grant-only `minimum`/`maximum`, with the explicit idempotency header. The receipt contains only pull number, channel, revision, epoch and outcome. Exact-key replay retains that result after later changes or restart, with current project write authorization still required, including after permission or credential revocation.

MCP `review_rounds_read` and project-write `review_rounds_configure` use the shared typed client, bound project/worker and strict declared arguments. MCP grant arguments use explicit minimum and maximum; exact means equal values. Neither tool refreshes source, starts a review or silently generates a key. A dispatched write with missing or invalid output is uncertain: retain its original binding, request and key for exact recovery.

## Browser current progress

`GET /api/v1/projects/{project_id}/reviews/rounds/progress?pull_numbers=31,32` reads the selected PRs' current native round controls in one authorized local transaction. Select one through one hundred distinct canonical positive 32-bit decimal PR numbers; unknown, repeated, empty or malformed selectors fail validation. Results retain requested PR order and include Hosted then CLI channels. Each channel explicitly reports whether a grant is configured, its revision and epoch, closure, minimum/maximum, consumed count, dry streak, taper threshold/completion and current-epoch in-flight operation count. Absent grants return unconfigured zero state. The compact response contains no actor attribution, imported source keys or provider capture, and performs no provider action or durable mutation.

In-flight operations include queued, preparing, posting, active and uncertain work, plus attributable complete results awaiting finalization, bound to that current grant epoch. They occupy capacity without becoming completed reviews. A remaining maximum includes that in-flight work; it is not a count of immediately available new requests. Zero in-flight work is not an Idle or Ready claim. Project policy, current comparison coverage, pending evidence and findings, provider limits and queue-front admission remain separate authorities.

The Delivery hero and queue share one bounded current-progress read for the visible PR membership. They display configured limits and taper beside each channel's separate retained run history. Current controls stay labelled separately from immutable review snapshots; an imported run does not create a native grant or completion credit. Failed reads cannot silently retain an unqualified old policy display. No grant is inferred from the number or outcome of shown historical rounds. Acceptance, queued-operation holds, grant changes and published native attempts/finalization invalidate the applicable active view; unopened history is never fetched to calculate progress. Complete selector and authorization checks apply independently of browser validation.

The PR badge remains **Review closed** when all configured channels allow no further rounds. Each stopped Hosted/CLI pill says **No reviews remaining**, with its reason available in expandable details: manually closed, maximum reached or taper met. These labels describe further review requests, not completion of finding work. Outstanding work stays separate; carried obligations without a retained channel identity remain at PR level rather than being assigned to Hosted or CLI.

## Focused proof

Real SQLite/domain normal and race tests cover grant baselines, close/reopen isolation, current authority and write revocation, CAS/replay, all live/uncertain states, capacity, minimum/taper, original finalized counts, late old-epoch completion and atomic failure. An actual ledger-write failure rolls back native finalization and receipt; exact-key retry counts once. Shared client/CLI checks use actual service handlers for strict scope, lost-response/stdout recovery and no-provider effects. Official SDK stdio checks cover the two tools, project write enforcement, replay and complete documented discovery.

The real local runner with a synthetic executable completed three additional CLI rounds on an already covered head and stopped at the default dry taper. Closing after preparation prevented another review process from starting. Focused race proof passed in 6.945 seconds; this is controlled execution, not live provider proof. Independent source review found a missing grant check inside transactional attempt admission; that check is now present, with a distinct close-between-preparation-and-admission regression. Current captured coverage, finished discovery and observed readiness remain separately tested.

Real backup creation/inspection/inactive restore covers a closed consumed grant, a new grant unaffected by late old-epoch completion, finalized accepted findings whose fix obligations remain pending, and genuine pre-grant captures that gain no additional credit. Resealed logical, key/type, audit, progress and schema/index corruption is refused before restore writes; older bundles without the optional domain remain supported. This validates the retained authority graph and corruption boundaries, not coherent rewriting of every authoritative native record. Operational migration and installed cutover are separate boundaries.

Actual packaged `fc`, `fcd` and official-SDK stdio `fcmcp` checks completed 23 CLI invocations and seven MCP calls in a 341 ms local workload. They exercised default/exact grants, cross-client original-key replay, PR/channel/project isolation, same-session credential demotion, uncertain stdout recovery and actual daemon restart. The workload made zero external/provider calls, created no review operations or completion credit, preserved unrelated table contents and left no owned process running. This is local functional proof, not sustained throughput, live provider, native-platform or installed-service proof. The canonical `make smoke` target includes this check.

## One explicit channel-front run

One `reviews start` invocation authorizes selection and at most one review in the requested channel. The service selects the earliest unfinished target in the configured order for that channel. A blocked front is a visible hold; another channel selects independently. A closed, tapered or exhausted grant can finish discovery while pending evidence/fix obligations still hold progression. Fresh verified merged members can be skipped. A closed unmerged PR without a deliberate finished discovery policy is a hold. An optional expected PR asserts the selection and never redirects it.

The explicit run is accepted locally as a durable finite selection request before provider reads. Acceptance binds the current execution configuration, policy and installed capability plus the authenticated actor and worker. Optional expected configuration revision is a caller assertion; omission pins the configuration at acceptance. Paused execution returns `reviews_paused`; an enabled service without the requested channel returns `review_not_configured`, consistently with exact-target admission. Selection performs bounded source reads outside HTTP and database transactions. It reads only the necessary queue prefix and comparison dependencies, coalescing repeated reads within that phase, and stops once the target is established. It does not fetch unrelated reviews, CI, historical provider captures or the queue suffix.

Transient local storage overload retries only the affected local admission, claim or completion phase with cancellable backoff from 100 ms to one second. Each transaction retains its existing bound. After source facts have been acquired, retries preserve those facts and recheck current authority and bindings before completion; they never repeat provider reads or a review action. Cancellation joins the workers promptly. Non-transient persistence failures remain fatal and pending selection recovery remains explicit at restart.

Selection rechecks current caller/worker authority, configuration, policy, capability, round state and unresolved native actions before creating one ordinary exact-head review operation. That insertion, the selection-to-operation link and compact metadata event commit atomically through shared native admission. A moved queue/configuration or expected-PR mismatch retains a hold without choosing a different target. Existing exact-target requests remain available and retain their current semantics. Preparation and final dispatch retain their independent identity, allowance and authorization checks.

Original-key replay returns the original selection identity and eventual operation link after restart or later queue movement. A lost response cannot select a second target. Repeated worker wakeups and read-only selection recovery create at most one linked review operation. Held/failed selection and completed reviews require a new explicit run to request another review. Uncertain native actions retain their reservations and established recovery path. Provider-disabled or restored-held services perform no selection provider work; explicit provider resumption abandons retained pending selections along with other pending provider work.

Run calls and reads share the established short wait, explicit longer wait and immediate-return options. CLI/API result waits and MCP `review_start` and `review_selection_read` allow 0–900 seconds; see the [host timeout guidance](agent-client.md#quiet-asynchronous-waiting). A pending result returns the original compact selection receipt; a useful completed result includes the selected review operation or a terminal selection hold/empty result. One bounded wait follows selection into its linked review operation when available, without forcing a separate agent polling loop. Wait preferences never change action identity, and cancellation detaches collection without canceling or repeating an admitted action. Selection and operation waits share bounded notification capacity and authorize every retained read.

Native admission implements those extensions. The source acquisition adapter still requires an obligation-clear canonical frame; a complete carried-frame exporter and installed cutover qualification remain separate work.

Retain original records privately; rebuilding commands for retrospective allocation/credit-period manipulation is outside the adoption requirement. A converter must report unsupported live behavior rather than silently substitute a different rule.

Map source behavior tests to functional outcomes before implementation: additional allowance baselines, minimum/exact requests, eligible dry streaks and reset, closure/reopen isolation, independent queue advancement, head/parent movement, exhausted-versus-taper status and outstanding findings. ## Prepared operational adoption

An administrator can adopt one explicitly selected effective PR/channel rule through a prepared native preview/apply operation. The prepared record carries remaining minimum and maximum, initial dry streak, taper threshold, reached taper and explicit closure, together with repository identity, source identity/key and source digest. It is configuration provenance, never a captured review or completed native round. Native `used` begins at zero. A carried CLI dry streak of two therefore needs one further eligible zero-accepted round to reach the usual threshold of three; a later accepted native round resets that streak normally.

Remaining maximum zero means already exhausted and does not grant a replacement round. The carried reached threshold remains explicit historical provenance and still waits for any remaining required minimum. On a deliberate cumulative edit, verified native streak contributions determine whether that earned proof remains valid; an accepted native completion resets it. Ordinary edits preserve the original imported baseline rather than erasing provenance. Closure, taper and exhaustion remain separate facts. Import does not satisfy current coverage, pending findings, CI or merge readiness.

Adoption initializes an unconfigured destination PR/channel only and may precede execution enrollment. Enrollment then preserves that source-owned rule. Preview binds the normalized record to the destination instance/project, current administrator/worker and expected control revision zero. Apply uses an explicit idempotency key and rechecks repository identity, current authority and absence of unresolved native operations for that PR/channel before committing control, original baseline, provenance, receipt and one compact round-setting event atomically. Same-key exact replay returns the original receipt after later native activity; changed input refuses. A different key cannot overwrite an adopted or subsequently edited control. Ordinary grants and close retain their existing authority and replay rules.

Finite prepared limits remain bounded to 1,000. Version-two adoption can instead preserve a source's absent maximum with `maximum_unbounded: true` and `maximum_remaining: 0`; this is distinct from an exhausted finite maximum. For that form, remaining minimum may reach the shared exact integer bound of 9,007,199,254,740,991; initial dry and taper retain their existing bounds of zero to 1,000 and one to 1,000 respectively. Native completions refuse before exceeding the exact counter bound. New ordinary grants remain finite and clear the flag. Unsupported source rules must refuse rather than substitute a cap or reset.

A source-specific helper must obtain effective counts and taper from the canonical source projection, prove either authoritative clearance or complete retained matching holds, refuse active/ambiguous or unchecked evidence, preserve its private input and digest, and report unsupported source behavior. The offline source preparer supports clearance frames and strictly bound carried-obligation frames; the live acquisition command retains its clearance-only boundary. Imported display history does not create native execution holds; unknown obligations and unfinalized counting effects must refuse operational adoption even when the source allowance is exhausted, tapered or closed. A sealed database and raw review-row counts alone do not establish that projection. Source export and conversion belong to optional tooling; the service accepts only the generic prepared record.

The bound administrator commands use an owner-private prepared file of at most 16 KiB:

```sh
fc reviews rounds adopt preview --file /private/import/control.json
fc reviews rounds adopt apply --file /private/import/control.json --preview SHA256 --key selected-control
```

These are setup commands rather than worker MCP mutations. The [optional source adapter guide](../tools/legacyimport/README.md#prepare-effective-review-controls) owns source input requirements and refusal boundaries.

Backup validation checks both the original carried baseline and subsequent genuine native completions, including ordinary closure and replacement grants. Upgrading a version-one round domain preserves its original rows/history and zero initial carry. The imported baseline never inserts native attempts, captures or completion rows. Focused native HTTP/client/CLI and backup tests cover carried accounting, guarded replay, version-one upgrade and corruption refusal. The optional source adapter has synthetic proof against explicitly selected copied helpers; the combined packaged control/message workload results, source checkpoints and qualification limits are recorded in the [adoption proof](legacy-adoption.md#executed-message-and-operational-adoption-proof).

Ordinary writer HTTP and engine checks cover grant, close, reopen, original-key replay and actor attribution without administrator authority. Read-only credentials/cookies, foreign project access, withdrawn write grants and revoked credentials refuse both new mutations and retained receipt recovery. Focused normal and race checks preserve revision, allowance and no-provider-action boundaries.

## Explicit selection proof

Focused real SQLite/worker, HTTP, typed CLI/client and official-SDK stdio checks cover one selection-to-operation link, original-key replay after restart/configuration changes, expected-PR refusal, current authority, grant changes, pending evidence, channel independence and atomic failure. Selection/result notification waits share the ordinary bounded wait capacity; a one-second HTTP regression follows delayed selection into the still-pending child within that same budget. At that proof checkpoint, a selected MCP wait at the then-current 50-second maximum remained pending beyond the ordinary call budget while another tool completed, and cancellation left durable work unchanged. The full adapter race suite passed in 55.928 seconds after aligning discovery, argument validation and the SDK fixtures to this limit.

Independent source review found that receiving routed findings could be bypassed after a front had captured coverage or finished rounds. Selection now checks those independent obligations both when classifying source and in the final link transaction. Genuine route/capture regressions fail without the guard and pass with it, including an incoming route created while source selection is in flight.

The first actual packaged Linux workload completed in 302 ms, with seven-ms enqueue, eight CLI invocations, two SDK MCP calls and exactly eight GitHub-shaped GETs tunneled only to an owned loopback HTTPS fixture. The packaged daemon selected the expected target past a closed grant and created one ordinary review operation, which deliberately held at a trusted failing Git stub before allowance or review execution. Expected-PR refusal, lost stdout, replay, later queue changes and daemon restart preserved the original link and source-call counts. No CLI allowance/review executable ran, no captured credit was created, input and unrelated-table data stayed unchanged, and owned processes stopped. This proves packaged selection into native admission; it does not establish a completed live provider review, other platforms, fleet throughput or installed cutover.

Real creation, inspection and inactive restore retain selected and pending selection state and its original operation link. Resealed schema, key/type, configuration and child-link corruption is refused before target writes. Explicit provider resumption atomically abandons pending selections with an audited count; a later reconciliation failure rolls back that abandonment. A subsequent real worker decision with otherwise valid authority makes no source call and creates no child for an abandoned selection. Older bundles/releases omit the new zero selection count.

The browser requests the read-only `rounds/progress?view=stages` projection. It retains total pending reservations and separately shows `pending_execution` (awaiting a review result) and `pending_adjudication` (completed retained results awaiting source-count finalization). Their sum equals pending; a completed review is not described as in flight merely because it is unadjudicated. Fix completion remains a separate finding obligation. Without `view=stages`, the original progress response is unchanged.
